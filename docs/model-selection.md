# Music embedding model selection

As of 2026-09-12, AutoDJ uses [`OpenMuQ/MuQ-large-msd-iter`](https://huggingface.co/OpenMuQ/MuQ-large-msd-iter) for music embeddings. MuQ remains a sound default because it accepts 24 kHz audio, produces a 1024-dimensional representation, and performed well across the music-information-retrieval tasks in the [MuQ paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01108). The available checkpoint is about 300 million parameters and uses CC-BY-NC-4.0 weights.

The paper results have an important limit. The public checkpoint was trained on the Million Song Dataset, while the paper evaluated a model trained with different and larger data. The model card warns that the public checkpoint might not reproduce the reported results. MARBLE and similar benchmarks also measure music understanding tasks, not whether two complete songs make a good transition. They support the current choice, but they do not prove that MuQ is the best model for AutoDJ.

Three released alternatives deserve a modest comparison:

- [`OpenMuQ/MuQ-MuLan-large`](https://huggingface.co/OpenMuQ/MuQ-MuLan-large) produces 512-dimensional audio embeddings and has promising recent evidence for perceptual audio similarity. A [2026 listening study](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19109) found a small advantage over one LAION-CLAP checkpoint for comparisons between different tracks. The study used five-second excerpts from 136 synthetic tracks, so its result might not transfer to full-song sequencing. MuQ-MuLan also has roughly twice as many parameters as MuQ and retains the noncommercial weight license.
- [MusicFM](https://github.com/minzwon/musicfm) is similar in size to MuQ and produces 1024-dimensional embeddings from 24 kHz audio. A [2026 recommendation study](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23077) found that it led one raw nearest-neighbor setting. That experiment averaged a listener's history and did not test transitions from the current song, so it is evidence for a trial rather than a replacement.
- [`laion/larger_clap_music_and_speech`](https://huggingface.co/laion/larger_clap_music_and_speech) provides 512-dimensional embeddings through Transformers and declares an Apache-2.0 license. Its 48 kHz input needs different preprocessing. Its license makes it worth evaluating if commercial use becomes a requirement; text-aligned training alone does not establish audio similarity quality.

Changing the configured model name does not switch architectures. `model.py` assumes the MuQ loader, 24 kHz input, final-layer pooling, and a 1024-dimensional output. `indexer.py` builds a 1040-dimensional FAISS vector after adding the 16 analysis features. Another architecture needs a compatible loader and preprocessing, with index dimensions adjusted if its output size differs. A change to the embeddings or pooling also requires a full reindex. Keep trials in a separate index so old and new vectors cannot mix.

Before switching, compare current MuQ with one challenger on a representative subset of the library. Keep the BPM, key, analysis features, and ranking logic fixed. Blindly judge the resulting candidate transitions, then record indexing time, memory use, model size, and failures on the hardware you use. These papers do not establish Windows AMD compatibility for the alternatives. A switch is justified only if the listening results improve enough to outweigh the added model and maintenance cost.
