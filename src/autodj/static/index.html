In Full intro and outro and Outro fade modes, tracks with detected markers fade for 1 to 12 seconds instead, even at 0.
              How long the incoming track takes to reach full volume. 0 =means incoming track starts at full volume immediately when the crossfade begins (no ramp-up). Capped at crossfade length.

              Transition effect
              Add an effect during crossfades. These effects are not used with saved segue points. Some transition effects need HTTPS. This page was opened over plain HTTP, so crossfades this browser plays skip gate stutter, bitcrusher, freeze and glitch: they play a plain crossfade, and random and rotate leave them out.
              How much of the transition effect sits on top of the plain crossfade, from 0 (plain crossfade only) to 100 (full effect).
              Transition effect details
              • echo out lets the ending song echo away
              • reverb tail lets a hall reverb linger after the song ends
              • high-pass sweep introduces the new track muffled then opens up
              • low-pass sweep filters the outgoing into a build
              • tape stop slows the outgoing to a halt over 4 s
              • gate stutter chops the ending song into rhythmic bursts
              • noise riser layers a synthesised white-noise build (bright)
              • noise drop opposite of the riser — bright crash falling to silence
              • backspin reverses the outgoing tail with vinyl-friction decel (2.0×→0.05×2.0 times to 0.05 times speed)
              • forward spin push-forward release (0.05×→2.5×0.05 times to 2.5 times speed accel)
              • cross-EQ swap hands bass duties from the outgoing to the incoming track
              • bitcrusher degrades bit-depth +and sample-rate (an old game-console sound)
              • flanger adds a swirly modulated delay
              • pitch swell bends the pitch upward
              • pitch fall sweeps pitch downward to 0.3× times (slowing the audio)
              • telephone 500-2800 Hz band-pass +and distortion for a phone-call sound
              • chorus three detuned voices for a thick double
              • submerge heavy lowpass +and reverb wash (underwater)
              • vinyl wow turntable pitch wobble ( ±plus or minus 25 % depth)
              • freeze captures the last 150 ms and loops it as it fades
              • glitch cuts the audio into short pieces +and reorders them
              • scratch turntablist 4-pass back-and-forth over a one-beat slice
              • beat repeat stamps a half-beat slice again and again across the tail (Pioneer Loop Roll)
              • sidechain pump rhythmic 4-on-the-floor amplitude duck on every beat
              • reverse reverb reverses a reverb tail so it swells into the transition
              • air horn square-wave horn sweeping up two octaves (220-880 Hz, or from the song's key with key-sync), layered with the music
              • vinyl rewind slow musical reverse with one-octave pitch drop (smoother than backspin)
              • transformer rapid 16-cps DJ-fader cut pattern with syncopated rhythm
              • dub siren sine 440-1760 Hz riser with 5 Hz vibrato (sits behind the music vs air horn's crash)
              • stutter build gate frequency accelerates 4 Hz → to 32 Hz across the fade for tension
              • wow +and flutter 1.5 Hz pitch wobble +and 8 Hz tremolo (worn-cassette feel, distinct from vinyl wow's pitch-only)
              • phaser adds a sweeping, hollow sound
              • ring modulator adds a metallic, bell-like tone
              • dub delay one-beat delay with lowpass on the feedback loop (each repeat darkens, classic Lee Perry sound)
              • halftime ramps tempo to 50 % keeping pitch intact (classic trap pre-drop, distinct from pitch fall)
              • auto picks one per crossfade to suit the two songs: a spin or a siren into a discovery pick, an echo, tape stop, reverb or filter to hide a clash of tempo or key, a riser or stutter build into a louder song, a clean EQ blend between songs in the same key and tempo
              • random picks one per crossfade
              • rotate cycles through every effect in order
              Time rhythmic effects to the songs’ beats and fit sweeps, risers and reverbs to whole bars. Uses ordinary timing when beat or tempo data is missing. For browser crossfades only.
              Tune air horn, dub siren and ring modulator to the songs’ root notes, moving from the old key to the new one during the fade. Without a detected key, each effect uses its usual pitch. For browser crossfades only.
              Skip
              When you press Skip, match the new song’s tempo during the crossfade without changing its pitch, up to a 15 percent adjustment. Otherwise it starts at its own tempo. For browser playback; stream and server audio use Skip style below.
              How Skip ends the playing track when the server does the mixing, in stream mode or with server audio. Quick fade (the default) fades it out in a fraction of a second. Echo out cuts the music and lets its echoes ring out. Backspin spins the record backwards to a stop. Loop roll repeats a short loop that rolls faster as it fades. Each effect lasts one to two beats, at most 2 seconds, and ends on a beat when the track's tempo is known; then the next track starts. Browser playback keeps its own skip.

              Match the new song’s tempo to the ending song during the crossfade, within the configured stretch limit (8 percent by default). For stream and server audio playback.

For stream and server audio playback.

              Timing
              Start the crossfade at a nearby musical phrase boundary. Uses the detected beat grid; without one, the track fades at its usual point. Works with browser, stream and server audio playback.
              How many bars make one phrase, a whole number from 1 to 64. 8 suits most dance music; 16 or 32 waits for longer phrases.
              Auto-detect each track's outro on first play and start the crossfade there, with the new track coming in at its first sound. For stream and server audio playback.
              When both tracks have synced lyrics, shorten or move the crossfade so the outgoing singer finishes before the incoming one starts. The fade is never made longer, or shorter than 1 second; when nothing works it stays as it was. On by default. For stream and server audio playback.
              Filter sweep
              Close a low-pass filter over the outgoing track during the crossfade, so it fades out muffled while the new track comes in clear. For stream and server audio playback.

              Song picking

              Picking
              Guide track selection toward a chosen tempo over time. For example, wakeup rises from 70 to 130 BPM and chill stays near 75 BPM. Add your own presets in config.toml.
              Similarity chooses a song that sounds like the last one. Random walk chooses at random. Least recently played prefers songs that have waited longest. Clockwheel follows the active clockwheel from Library tools, restarting it at a song handoff near its chosen minute marks.
              How many of the closest-sounding songs a similarity pick chooses among, a whole number from 1 to 100. 1, the default, always takes the closest one.
              How evenly a pick spreads over those closest songs, from 0 to 10. 0 always takes the closest; higher numbers give the others more of a chance. No effect while the number above is 1. The default is 0.3.
              Restrict next-track picks to harmonically related keys. compatible is the classic Camelot rule (same position, ±plus or minus 1, or relative major/ or minor) — broadest pool. strict only allows the exact same key. neighbour walks the wheel one step at a time, no mode flips. mood change swaps major↔ and minor only (energy stays, mood flips). energy boost picks two steps up the wheel — each track is two semitones up, so the set lifts over time. Pick off to disable the harmonic filter completely.
              Keep similarity picks close to the song that started the session, instead of following each new song. Discovery can still choose a different-sounding track.
              Choose where similarity picks resume after your queue finishes: from the last queued song, or from the song that was playing when you started the queue.
              Repeats

              The artist, album, track, title and related rules each have a Tracks limit and a Minutes limit; when both are set, a repeat waits for both. 0 turns a limit off. Tracks are whole numbers, up to 100000 for the same track and up to 5000 for the others. Minutes go up to 10080 (one week) and can have decimals. The daypart and quarter-hour rules, last, keep a song from playing at the same time every day. When no song passes the rules, they give way for that pick rather than stop the music.

              Output processing
              Off leaves levels unchanged. ReplayGain adjusts tracks using their loudness tags. Smooth balances source levels and compresses the mix. Stereo Tool balances sources and runs the mix through the Stereo Tool preset chosen below; its executable must be configured on the server. For stream and server audio playback.
              The loudness every tagged track is brought to, from minus 30 to 0. Minus 14 is the default; minus 18 is the original ReplayGain reference and plays quieter. For stream and server audio playback.
              ReplayGain never raises a track so far that its tagged peak goes above this level, from 0.1 to 2. 1, the default, prevents clipping; above 1 allows some. For stream and server audio playback.
              Raise a song's quiet opening by up to this much, from 0 to 6, so it does not sound too low after a loud song. The lift holds for one second, then falls back to the song's own level by eight seconds, and never raises the opening's peaks above 1.8 decibels below full scale. 4 is the default; 0 turns it off. A song is measured on its next library scan. For ReplayGain playback.
              Stereo Tool

              Stereo Tool restarts with the chosen preset; the sound bypasses it for a moment while it does. Included is the free preset that comes with AutoDJ and cannot be deleted. Uploaded presets are kept with the station's data and saved in backups.
              Lower or raise the mix before Stereo Tool, from minus 12 to plus 6. Lower it when the preset sounds squashed; raise it when the preset barely acts. The output limiter still holds the same ceiling. 0 is the default. For stream and server audio playback.
              Controls how strongly Stereo Tool brings up quieter passages. 7 dB is the default. 0 keeps the selected preset's original drive; it does not turn AGC off. Changing this reloads Stereo Tool, with a brief bypass while it warms up. For stream and server audio playback.

                                              Library jobs

                                              Index rescans your registered folders and analyses eligible songs in the current library. Enrich updates its metadata from beets. Analyse, Prune and Stats below are legacy tools for older index files; they do not update the current library.

                                              Add songs from your registered folders, then analyse those with advanced analysis enabled. The optional limit caps advanced analysis; all songs are added first. Existing cues, play history and settings are kept.

                                              Enrich refreshes title/artist/genre/BPM/key from beets without re-embedding. Analyse finds the intro, outro, beat grid and cue points of tracks not analysed yet; already analysed tracks are skipped, so running it again is quick. Prune drops index entries whose files were deleted. Stats prints the full library report from the indexer. Stop terminates the running subprocess.

                                              Categories

                                              A category is a set of songs that a clockwheel step picks from, such as rotation A, B, C and D, or Remembering the 2000s. Its songs come from a beets query or from an M3U playlist file. AutoDJ works the songs out again when beets, a playlist file or the library changes.

                                              Songs come from

                                              Any beets field, including ones plugins add, such as original_year:2000..2009 or genre:rock ^artist:"bon jovi". field:value matches part of the text, a..b is a range of numbers, ^ leaves out what follows it, and quotes keep words together; every part must match. original_year is the first release, and the release year stands in when beets has none. Regular expressions, alternatives with commas and plugin queries are not supported.

                                              M3U and M3U8 files in the playlists folder. Each line is matched to a song in the library, by its full path or by a path from the playlist's folder or the music folder; lines that match none are counted as missing.

                                              When the songs may play

                                              Optional. Outside these days, hours and dates no pick mode plays the category's songs, and a clockwheel step for it is skipped. A song in several categories plays only when all of them allow it. Leave every day unchecked for every day, both times empty for all day, and both dates empty for every day of the year; both dates are included, such as 1 December until 26 December for seasonal songs. Times and dates are in the station time zone, and a window that crosses midnight, such as 22:00 until 02:00, belongs to the day it starts. A song you queue yourself plays at any time.

                                              Clockwheels

                                              A clockwheel is a repeating order of steps: music or voice liners from a library folder, music from a category, a specific track, or a preset change. Keep as many as you like. The active one plays when Pick mode is set to Clockwheel, and the Schedule can switch to another one at set times.

                                              Saved clockwheels

                                              • No clockwheels loaded.

                                              Up to 64 letters, digits, spaces, dashes and underscores. Create, Duplicate, Rename and Import .PAL use this name.

                                              Import makes a new clockwheel from a SAM Broadcaster .PAL file. Only Dir and SubDir queue lines are read, and nothing in the file runs. Each folder must be a registered music or voice liner folder, or a folder inside one that adds subfolders.

                                              Steps of no clockwheel open

                                              Up and Down move between step names; Left and Right move between row buttons; Home and End move to the first and last row buttons. Enter or Space presses the focused button, so Enter on a step name edits it; Delete removes the step, just like its Delete button. Delete and Undo step change are unavailable while editing a step. Ctrl+Z or Undo step change restores the last step change until you save or open another clockwheel. Alt+Up and Alt+Down move the focused step. Changes are kept only when you press Save clockwheel.

                                              Days

                                              Comma separated, 0 to 59, such as 0, 30. The clockwheel goes back to step 1 at the song change nearest each minute. Leave empty to restart only when the wheel wraps.

                                              Continuing keeps the next step number. Moving or deleting steps can change what plays there. If that number no longer exists, the clock starts at step 1.

                                              Keyboard shortcuts

                                              Space and the Up and Down Arrow keys work on the Now Playing tab only, because on other tabs they scroll the page. Every other shortcut works from any tab. Letter and punctuation shortcuts work on focused buttons and sliders, but not while typing in a text field or using a dropdown. Space activates a focused button; arrow keys operate the focused slider or tab.
